HOW TO READ THIS FORECAST

What the ratings mean, and where the numbers come from

Marine Weather Jamaica turns computer-model forecasts into a simple rating for each area. This page explains how, so you can judge it for yourself.

Not for navigation. This is forecast guidance, not an official warning service. Always check the Meteorological Service of Jamaica for small craft warnings and advisories before you go out, and use your own judgement on the water.

The three ratings

RatingWhat it meansStarting point (average over the window)
GOODGenerally comfortable conditions for small boats.Seas up to 1.5 m (about 4.9 ft) and wind up to 12 kt
CAUTIONWorkable, but expect a rougher ride. Smaller vessels should think twice.Seas up to 2.0 m (about 6.6 ft) and wind up to 18 kt
ALERTRough or risky conditions. Check official advisories and consider staying in.Anything rougher than CAUTION

The rating is then moved up one level (worse) for each of these:

  • Short, choppy seas: wave period of 3.5 seconds or less (two levels at 2.5 seconds or less).
  • Thunderstorms in the forecast.
  • Heavy rain: 20 mm or more in the day, or a high chance of rain (80%+) with 15 mm or more.
  • Squalls: strong gusts combined with heavy rain in the same hour.

These thresholds are set in the forecast scripts and may be fine-tuned over time.

The “Why” line on each card shows the main reason a rating is CAUTION or ALERT, so you can decide whether it matters for your boat and your trip.

Inshore and offshore are rated separately

The outlook rates Kingston / Maiden Cay (inshore) and the offshore banks — currently Mackerel (Bowditch) and California Bank — on their own. Conditions offshore are often rougher than in the harbour.

The coloured line under each day button shows the worst rating of all three areas that day. If any bank is rated worse than Kingston, a warning banner appears at the top. A green Kingston rating never hides an offshore alert.

Comfort score (1–5)

The comfort score describes how pleasant the ride is likely to be, where 5 is the calmest. It mostly reflects sea height, then wind speed, and drops for short, choppy seas. It is a comfort guide, not a safety rating — always read it together with the GOOD / CAUTION / ALERT rating.

Forecast windows

  • Daily outlook: 07:00–17:00 Jamaica time for each day. Days 1–3 are short-range forecasts. Days 4–7 are extended outlooks and are much less certain.
  • Bank forecast: “Next 24 hours” starts from the current hour; “Tomorrow” covers 07:00–17:00. The rating badge on a bank covers the next few hours (or tomorrow’s daytime), and the charts show every hour.
  • Weather windows: stretches of at least two hours when conditions look workable. They are guidance only, not route or vessel clearance.

Units

  • Wind and gusts in knots (kt). Gusts are the strongest bursts expected in that hour.
  • Seas in feet (ft) — the average wave height, not the biggest single wave. Occasional waves can be noticeably bigger.
  • Wave period in seconds (s) — the time between waves. Short periods (under about 6 s) mean a steeper, choppier ride.
  • Current in knots, with the direction it flows towards.
  • All times are Jamaica time.

Where the data comes from

  • Weather and marine forecasts: Open-Meteo (licensed under CC BY 4.0), which combines several national weather models.
  • Backup weather data and sun and moon times: Visual Crossing.
  • Tides are modelled for Kingston and are approximate elsewhere.
  • Map: © OpenStreetMap contributors.

These are global computer models. They are not checked against local buoys or reports from fishermen, and they can miss small, fast-changing features such as local squalls and currents around the banks. If what you see on the water doesn’t match the forecast, trust what you see.

How fresh is it?

The 7-day outlook is refreshed every morning at about 06:45, and bank forecasts about every three hours. Each card shows when it was last updated. If the outlook is more than a day old, the page shows a warning.
